# Study notes guidelines

These are the family's rules for every study note in the app: history, geography, science, original-language words, the main Christian views, and scholars. Anyone adding notes follows them.

## Why this app exists
To help the family grow in faith together: to love the Lord our God with all our heart and to love others (Matthew 22:37-39), to understand God's grace through Jesus, forgiveness and the hope of salvation, and to live it out every day. It is a gift for every age, from grandchildren to great-grandparents.
- The Bible, Jesus and our faith stay at the center.
- World religions, history, science and apologetics are side resources. Use them mainly where they help us understand or strengthen faith, and keep them brief so they never take over the study.

## The Bible is what we study
- Scripture is the guide. Notes inform. They don't steer the reader toward a side.
- Where the Bible speaks plainly, say so plainly and quote it.
- Where the Bible is silent, say it is silent. Don't fill the gap with tradition or guesses.
- Where faithful readers differ, say it's a matter of interpretation and show the main views fairly.
- Never change or soften the words of Scripture. Quote the translation exactly.
- Where the Bible is clear (for example, stealing is wrong, adultery is wrong), say so clearly. Don't water it down.

## Where Christians differ: stay neutral
This app is for learning and growing, not for winning arguments. The family includes people who hold different views, and it must never make anyone feel pushed.
- Say plainly that there is more than one view held by faithful Christians.
- Give each main view fairly, in words its own holders would accept: who holds it (scholars, churches, traditions) and why.
- Say where each view's support comes from: the Bible's own words, the original language, history, archaeology, or science.
- Don't declare a winner. Where some support is stronger, it's fine to say what the evidence is, but leave the conclusion to the reader.
- End on the common ground everyone shares.
- Example, the days of creation (Genesis 1): some hold six 24-hour days and a young earth; some hold six real days with a creation that appears mature or aged by processes we don't fully understand; some read the days as long ages or a literary framework and accept an old earth. All of these Christians agree that God is the Creator of everything.
- Literal and figurative readings, and denominational views (Baptist, Church of Christ and others), can all be shown, each named for what it is.

## Stay on topic
- No politics, parties or current controversies.
- The family is Baptist and Church of Christ. Other faiths (for example Mormon, Jehovah's Witness, Muslim) are mentioned only briefly, to inform, when it helps understand a passage or a question someone asks. Don't spend study time on their doctrines.
- Never mocking, never divisive. The goal is to grow hearts and minds and understand others kindly.

## Label every note
- **Scripture says**: the text states it directly (give the verse).
- **Strong evidence**: ancient texts, archaeology or measurement that scholars of all views accept.
- **Good evidence**: well supported, with some details still discussed.
- **Debated**: careful scholars disagree. Show the main views without picking a winner.
- **Faith reading**: a way believers read the text that goes beyond what evidence can show.
- **Scripture is silent**: the Bible doesn't say. Note what people have suggested, and label it as suggestion.

## Name every source and where it comes from
- Every note lists its sources, with author, title, publisher and year.
- Say who the scholar is and what tradition they write from, for example: Baptist, Church of Christ (Restoration Movement), Catholic, Jewish, secular historian, young-earth or old-earth creationist.
- When a view belongs to a particular church, group or organization, name that group.
- Church of Christ and Baptist views are shown clearly and respectfully, as the family's own background, alongside other views, not in place of them.
- Authors the family enjoys: C. S. Lewis, Max Lucado, Josh Ross, and careful apologetics writers (for example Lee Strobel's The Case for Christ). Quote them where they truly help, and name them.

## Science
- Science notes show connections and support for apologetics, and where findings line up with Scripture.
- Science never overrides Scripture in these notes. Where science and a reading of Scripture seem to disagree, show both honestly and say which part is measurement and which part is interpretation.

## Other religions
- Explain plainly where other faiths differ and why, so we can talk kindly with people outside our faith.
- Describe other faiths the way their own scholars would recognize. No mocking.

## For grandparents and Bible class
- Plain words, short paragraphs, large clear text. Nothing alarming or cluttered.
- The aim is to deepen faith, answer honest questions, and give Bible class teachers material they can use, not to unsettle anyone.
