Bible keys and versions (set up once)

Versions the family can switch to

Everything that's on shows in the version button at the top of the Read page for everyone. Each person picks their own favorite there, or in Settings, under You.

Signed in as the family organizer, tapping Save shares the ESV key with the whole family. Family members never see it, and keys are never put in backups. Until an ESV key is saved, everyone reads the NASB and the other versions as usual, and the ESV simply doesn't appear in the version switch.

Set up a device for someone without an account

This makes a private invite link. When they open it on their phone or iPad, the app saves your key there so they can start reading right away. They use the same 5,000 reads a month as you, so send it only to someone you trust, by text or email.

How to get your free Bible key (about 5 minutes)
  1. On your phone or tablet, open api.bible and tap Sign up.
  2. Choose the free Starter plan and make an account with your own email. Check your email and confirm it.
  3. When it asks about your app, name it Bible Study and say it's personal, non-commercial use.
  4. If it asks which translations you want, include NASB.
  5. Copy your API key (a long code), come back here, and paste it into API.Bible key above.
  6. Tap Load versions on my plan to check that it works.

The free plan allows 5,000 chapters a month, far more than one person reads. For the ESV, get a free key the same way at api.esv.org.

About and family

About this app

A free Bible study made for our family, not for sale.

  • The Bible text belongs to its publishers. Each chapter shows its copyright line at the bottom. The ESV is from Crossway, used with their permission for non-commercial use. The NASB is © The Lockman Foundation. Other versions come through API.Bible.
  • Recorded ESV narration is © Crossway.
  • The study buddy answers with AI (Claude, by Anthropic). It tries hard to be accurate, but it can make mistakes. Check what it says against the Bible, and ask a pastor or teacher about anything important.
  • The study notes show what respected Bible scholars have written, with their sources listed. Where scholars differ, more than one view is shown. They aren't any church's official teaching.
  • Cross-references and Bible places (where each place was, how sure scholars are, and its name today) come from OpenBible.info (CC BY). Greek and Hebrew word tagging comes from STEPBible.org, Tyndale House Cambridge (CC BY), with definitions from James Strong (1890).
  • Paintings in Classic 3 are by David Roberts (1839), from the Cleveland Museum of Art's open collection.
  • Your privacy: your journal and notes stay on your device and in our family's own account. Keys are never put in backups.
